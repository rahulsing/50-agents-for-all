# City of Riverton - Urban Mobility Review 2026 (fictional sample report)

## 1. Introduction
This review assesses how residents of Riverton (population 610,000) travelled during 2025, compares results with the 2021 baseline, and sets priorities for 2026-2030. Data comes from ticketing systems, 42 automatic traffic counters, a household travel survey of 8,200 residents, and the bike-share operator.

## 2. How people travel
Car trips fell from 58% of all trips in 2021 to 51% in 2025. Public transport rose from 22% to 25%, cycling from 4% to 8%, and walking stayed at 16%. The largest change was among people aged 18-34, whose car use fell by 14 percentage points. Residents of the outer districts still make 71% of their trips by car because bus frequency there is often only every 30 minutes.

## 3. Public transport
Bus ridership reached 61 million boardings, 9% above 2021. The new tram line (opened March 2024) carried 14 million passengers, beating its forecast of 11 million. Customer satisfaction rose to 72% (from 64%). However, punctuality on the 12 busiest bus routes fell to 81% because of road congestion and roadworks, and the bus fleet is old: 40% of buses are more than 12 years old.

## 4. Cycling and walking
The protected cycle network grew from 38 km to 96 km. Bike-share trips doubled to 2.3 million. Collisions involving cyclists fell 18% despite more cycling. Walking conditions remain uneven: 27% of survey respondents said pavements in their area are in poor condition, mainly in the north-east.

## 5. Roads and congestion
Average peak-hour speeds in the centre rose slightly from 17 to 19 km/h after the introduction of the low-traffic zone. On the ring road, congestion worsened: average delay per trip increased from 6 to 9 minutes. Freight deliveries grew 15% with the growth of online shopping, and delivery vans now make up 13% of central traffic.

## 6. Emissions and health
Transport CO2 emissions fell 11% compared to 2021. Nitrogen dioxide levels at the 5 central monitoring stations dropped below the legal limit for the first time. Two stations next to the ring road still exceed the limit.

## 7. Money
The city spent 212 million USD on transport in 2025: 48% on public transport operations, 22% on road maintenance, 18% on the tram, 9% on cycling and walking, 3% on studies and staff. Fare revenue covered 46% of public transport operating costs. A national grant of 60 million USD for electric buses is available if the city applies before June 2026.

## 8. Recommendations
1. Raise outer-district bus frequency to every 10-15 minutes on the 6 busiest routes.
2. Apply for the electric bus grant and replace the oldest 120 buses by 2028.
3. Add bus priority lanes on the 12 least punctual routes.
4. Extend the cycle network to 150 km, focusing on the north-east.
5. Create consolidated delivery hubs to cut van traffic in the centre.
6. Launch a 30 million USD pavement repair programme.

## 9. Conclusion
Riverton has made real progress: fewer car trips, record tram use, safer cycling and cleaner central air. The main gaps are poor bus service in outer districts, ageing buses, ring-road congestion and freight growth. The electric bus grant deadline in June 2026 is the most time-critical decision.
